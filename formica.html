The nest tracks the colony both ways. Crowding drives excavation and surplus space drives backfilling, with a wide gap between the two so neither chases the other. A cell is only filled when its excavated neighbours form a single run around it — a condition that provably cannot sever a gallery or seal the entrance, and one the simulation checks by walking every nest cell from the mouth on each sample.

Dormancy comes from inside the colony. L. niger runs an endogenous annual cycle: after spring reactivation the queen has a fixed period, and when it elapses she stops — in late summer, well before it turns cold. Chilling is what arms reactivation; spring warmth triggers it. So winter cannot be skipped, only spent warm, and a colony held at summer temperature sits dormant at full metabolic rate, ageing and burning reserves while producing nothing. Nothing penalises it for that; the cost is only arithmetic.

The year turns on its own. Temperature follows a sinusoid from 7°C in midwinter to 25°C at the peak, and one scalar — how fast an ant does anything — carries it into every rate. Nothing has a winter switch. Digging slows because diggers move slowly, which is what the excavation work found: ants dig the same nests, faster, when warm. Workers bank fat in autumn and live off it; larvae that are too cold to develop are too cold to be hungry, so a cohort overwinters instead of starving.

Brood sorts itself into rings. There are no egg, larva and pupa rooms. An ant carries an item a random direction by a distance that depends only on what it is; small items diffuse least and stay central, and isolated items are the ones most likely to be picked up, which pulls inward. That balance alone produces concentric annuli — eggs innermost, pupae intermediate, larvae outermost. No template, no target radius, no assigned chambers.

The nest is dug, not drawn. Workers start in the chamber a founding queen excavated and enlarge it themselves. Nobody measures the nest: an ant digs when the space around her is crowded, and enlarging it spreads everyone out, which stops the digging. Volume proportional to population falls out of that loop — measured at 2.5 to 3.6 cells per ant across a fourfold change in colony size. Spoil is hauled to the mouth, which is where the mound comes from.

No global food variables. Carbohydrate lives in individual crops and moves by trophallaxis, where the volume passed is set by the receiver's empty space. Larval hunger reaches foragers one nurse-to-larva contact at a time, and the queen begs her attendants when the larder runs dry. No ant, including her, can read a colony total. The crop and larder figures below are derived readouts only — nothing in the model reads them back.

One program per caste. Every worker runs the same WORKER_PROGRAM; the queen runs QUEEN_PROGRAM. Castes, trails, traffic, brood placement and colony size are emergent, not scripted. Aphid colonies establish, peak and senesce on their own; prey turns up at random. Nothing here needs tending. Rules are annotated in source with the finding they came from.
